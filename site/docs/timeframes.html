Timeframes

The whole pipeline — data, search, metrics, portfolio — follows one bar-size setting. Everything that depends on the bar size (annualization, volatility windows, calendar grid, funding aggregation) is adjusted automatically; daily behaviour reproduces the classic daily engine exactly.

Recommended windows

Finer bars are denser and heavier, so the recommended history window shrinks as bars get finer while still spanning several market regimes. Picking a timeframe in the GUI fills these in as editable defaults:

Crypto trades 24/7, so a year is 365 calendar days of bars — Sharpe ratios across timeframes are annualized consistently and stay comparable.

Per-timeframe isolation

Each bar size keeps its own data snapshot, its own alpha library and its own search history. Alphas mined on 1h bars never mix with daily ones — a formula's metrics always mean "on this bar size".

Two engines, one meaning

AlphaNode deliberately runs two simulator implementations:

WhereEngineWhy
Search, leaderboard, intraday portfolio fastsim — a compiled numpy port ~100× faster; its numbers are exactly what the search optimized, on any bar size.
Daily portfolio, paper bundle, signal API the real pandas engine Matches the paper/serve products bar-for-bar.

The two agree closely (return correlation ≥ 0.99; the portfolio layer adds a second vol-targeting pass, so weights can differ by a few percent) — and the GUI always labels which engine produced the numbers you're looking at.

Funding is a first-class feature

Perpetual funding payments are downloaded with the price history and aggregated into each bar as a flow. They enter the simulation P&L (longs pay positive funding) and are available to formulas as the funding terminal — so carry harvesting and funding-momentum alphas are part of the search space, on every timeframe.
